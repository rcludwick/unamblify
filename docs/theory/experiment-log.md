# Experiment log

Every experiment run so far, what it varied, and what it settled. Scores are `eval/lsd` at the run's own best checkpoint unless stated otherwise. Lower is better. Read [how big a difference counts as real](reading-the-numbers.md#statistical-significance) first: 0.05 is noise, 0.25 is signal.

!!! info "Provenance"
    Runs live under `$UNAMBLIFY_DATA/runs/`. The measurement scripts and logs are on the 8 TB data drive. Dates are when the experiment concluded.

## The vocoder chain

| # | Date | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | 2026-09-11 | Do YSF DN and DMR need separate captures? | No. They share 49 identical voice bits. The difference is in framing and FEC outside the vocoder payload. One `ysf-dmr` capture serves both, allowing DMR to be retired as a capture mode. |
| 5 | 2026-09-11 | Can the ThumbDV go faster than 460 800 baud? | No, that is the chip's maximum. However, baud rate was not the bottleneck, as 7.07 ms of each frame is the 326-byte speech packet's wire time. |
| 6 | 2026-09-12 | Can the two directions overlap, since the UART is full duplex? | Yes, yielding a 1.41× end-to-end speedup. Keeping 3 encodes and 3 decodes in flight increased the harness from 68.9 to 97 frames/s. An isolated probe showed 1.73×. Real utterances also incur warm-up and turnaround costs. A depth of 4 + 4 stalls the chip, so depth is fixed rather than tunable. |
| 27 | 2026-09-19 | How good is an open D-STAR vocoder against the chip? (Bruce Perens' `ham_digital_modes`, from-spec AMBE, vendored as `vendor/ham-digital-modes`) | Chip 2.56, software 2.18 (UTMOS22 over all 1 298 dev utterances captured in D-STAR, clean 4.10). This is not a level artefact (mean RMS 0.0481 / 0.0429 against clean 0.0479) or a framing issue. Both produce identical frame counts and durations on real speech. It provides a second implementation and a different D-STAR degradation than a radio produces. It serves as a sibling capture set (`dstar+perens`, 60 090 utterances) rather than a replacement. AMBE+2 is not vendored due to active patents. |
| 29 | 2026-09-19 | What does a badly driven microphone cost through the codec, and is a quiet talker just quiet? (150 dev clips of each kind through Codec 2 3200, UTMOS22) | Clean 3.15, normal level 1.90, overdriven (`+h`) 1.52, underdriven (`+u`, 15–30 dB down) 1.38, and 1.42 with the level restored afterwards (+21.9 dB mean). A gain stage after the decoder recovers 0.04 of the 0.52 lost. The degradation occurs inside the vocoder, which quantises weak signals poorly. The output level is not the primary issue, which is why `+u` twins are not renormalised (the low level acts as the augmentation). Both kinds are trained as twins (damaged input through the codec, clean parent as target) instead of using a gain or de-clipper before the model. 21 342 `+h` and 21 229 `+u` twins are prepared. No noisy (`+n`) twins exist yet. |
| 30 | 2026-09-20 | Is the existing frame-loss data enough to train restoration on? | No. The first Codec 2 `drops` pass was 2 % with `mute`. In a 20 000-row sample, 5 596 utterances (28 %) lose no frames. A second pass at 10 % (bursts of 1–4, `--subst erase` for parameter-domain fill) added 60 000 rows per mode. Of those, 360 (3200) and 2 515 (1600) remain untouched. Using `shard --min-drop-rate 0.05` isolates that pass. On the model side, `[model] erasure_in` feeds the lag-aligned per-frame mask into the context conv so gaps are identified early. AMBE drops require the chip for decoding and are not yet captured. No run has trained on this data. |

## Naturalness

| # | Date | Question | Answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 15 | 2026-09-12 | Why does it still sound robotic once the high band is restored? | The output is too periodic. Harmonic peaks over inter-harmonic troughs on voiced frames show: clean speech 18.96 dB, D-STAR decode 29.21 dB, filter-only model 24.52 dB (+2.22 excess, measured on clean's voiced frames). Codec 2 3200 shows 18.82 / 23.00 / 21.87. The codec empties the valleys between harmonics where breath, jitter and glottal noise reside. A filter cannot restore them. |
| 16 | 2026-09-12 | Why could the model not learn to fix it? | There are two reasons. Capability: a per-frame FIR and comb can only reshape existing signals, and filtering a periodic signal leaves it periodic. Objective: spectral losses compare magnitudes against a single clean recording. Since outputs cannot match specific realisations of breath noise, the model smooths the output. Both areas required changes. |
| 17 | 2026-09-12 | Does a cepstral periodicity loss make the model use the noise head? | No. At `periodicity_w = 0.5` the term was 0.08 % of the total loss and had no effect. At 50 it was gamed: prominence error dropped to 0.01 while the harmonic-to-trough ratio worsened (24.6 → 26.0 dB) and LSD lost two points. Cepstral prominence shifts with ripple at neighbouring quefrencies without filling troughs. |
| 18 | 2026-09-12 | Does measuring the thing directly work? | The mean improves. `[train] hnr_w = 0.1` measures the harmonic-to-trough ratio in dB using pitch and bin masks from the target. It reduced the mean excess from +2.22 to +0.12 dB (+1.5 to −0.9 per harmonic) with no LSD cost (converged medians 11.00 vs baseline 11.01). The cepstral column corroborated this independently at weight 0, and `eval/lsd_rx` improved 0.75 dB (~1.1σ). See item 21 for limitations. |
| 21 | 2026-09-12 | Review: is the parity real? | No, it is a mean rather than a match. Per frame, the noise-head model's excess spans −5 to +3 dB (p10–p90) against a ±1.2 dB floor. The slope on the clean's frame-by-frame ratio is 0.82. 0–1 kHz is over-filled by 1.1 dB and 2–4 kHz is unaffected. `loss/hnr_l1` shifted from 4.4 to 4.0. The loss and metric used `mean(peaks)/mean(troughs)`, while the noise was a constant share of RMS without modulation. Updated 2026-09-13 to use per-harmonic averaging, `eval/hnr_abs`, envelope-modulated noise with learned depth (`[model] noise_mod`), and interpolated frame gains. |
| 22 | 2026-09-12 | Review: are plosives preserved? | They are passed through but not restored, and the closure is filled. Codec metrics: burst −12.8 dB, rise 3 → 14 ms, length 10 → 30 ms. Both models perform identically on the burst and are 3 dB shallower in the closure. Every per-frame gain was held 10 ms with a finest loss window of 32 ms. Updated to include `eval/plosive_*`, `[train] transient_w` (1 ms high-band envelope + slope), and 1 ms noise gains. |
| 23 | 2026-09-12 | Review: where did the AMBE chip time go? | 58 % was allocated to `dev` and `test`. LibriTTS-R dev/test subsets were held out entirely. D-STAR had 12.0 h in train and 20.8 h held out for an eval of 17 clips. The split rule now hashes readers to pin eval data. `prepare --resplit` shifted 8 228 utterances to train, and `mixed-large-v2` was rebuilt. `[data] mode_weights` replaces the 84 % Codec 2 batch share. |
| 24 | 2026-09-13 | What does a learned naturalness judge say? | Clean 4.05, codec 1.78, model 2.08 (UTMOS22 over 219 clips. D-STAR 1.88 → 2.20). Spectral metrics largely measured simpler aspects. `scripts/mos.py` is now the standard metric. |
| 25 | 2026-09-19 | `mixed-50k-v4-ysf-heavy-ll5`: 200 k set, 40 000 steps, `hnr_w` 0.1. Did twice the data help? | No. MOS reached 1.91 (below the 2.08 target), while `eval/lsd` dropped 14.97 → 11.74 and mel 9.12 → 6.44. Spectral metrics indicated success while the MOS judge did not. Two factors contributed: loss terms (except `hnr`) were still decreasing, meaning the run had not converged. Also, `loss/hnr` plateaued mid-run, and `ckpt.keep = 5` deleted early checkpoints where `hnr` and `plosive_burst` peaked. |
| 26 | 2026-09-19 | `…-long`: same set, 80 000 steps, `hnr_w` 0.3, `keep = 40`. | MOS 2.12 at step 76 000 against degraded 1.79. This improves by +0.33 compared to v4's +0.12. Mode breakdowns at 76 k: ysf-dmr 1.98 → 2.49, dstar 2.11 → 2.39, codec2-3200 1.73 → 2.12, codec2-1600 1.33 → 1.46. `hnr_abs` was 2.835, and `lsd_rx` hit 14.52. Checkpoints should be retained since the best MOS did not align with the best `hnr_abs`. |
| 31 | 2026-09-20 | `mixed-50k-v5-perens-ll5`: v4's recipe plus `dstar+perens` and a 12 % twin share. What do a second D-STAR implementation and twins buy? | Results were poor for D-STAR due to a build bug. Overall MOS was 1.89 against 2.12. D-STAR output scored 1.48 (below the 2.11 chip decode). Other modes matched v4. The shard builder retained one lag per mode and truncated `dstar+perens` at the chip's 326 samples, while the software vocoder lags by 216. Half the D-STAR examples were misaligned by 110 samples, causing the model to learn a smeared output. This issue bypassed spectral metrics (`eval/lsd@dstar` was 11.39) but affected MOS. Fixed by implementing per-capture-set lags in `index.json`. |
| 32 | 2026-09-20 | Did training on twins help on badly driven speech? | It prevented harm but offered no improvement. Raw decode: overdriven 1.89, underdriven 2.10. v4 output: 1.89 and 1.89 (worsening quiet speech by 0.21). v5 output: 1.90 and 2.08. On normal speech, both models lift the decode by 0.5 points. The architecture struggles here since a per-frame FIR cannot invert clipping or re-quantise weak signals. Twins are retained as training data to avoid damaging quiet talkers. |
| 33 | 2026-09-20 | Where is the gap? Zero-training probes on eval clips splitting mel at 3.8 kHz. | The low band shows no gains. Low from codec: 1.70 (no high band), 2.76 (clean high). Low from v4 model: 2.36 (own high), 2.72 (clean high, similar to raw codec's 2.76). Low from clean: 3.08 / 3.50 (model high) / 3.75 (clean). Existing improvements stem from the bandwidth-extension head. Resynthesising the codec's mel with natural phase scores 1.70 against 1.79, indicating the robotic quality lies in mel-resolution magnitudes. The filter architecture cannot resolve this; a feature restorer followed by a vocoder is required (see [training](../design/training.md#candidate-3-concretely)). |

## What is still open

!!! note "2026-09-25"
    This list dates from the filter model (candidate 1). Since #33 the work has moved to the restorer + waveform synthesiser pipeline (candidate 3), which scores 3.57 predicted MOS against the filter's 2.12 (#38). Open questions relate to the sibilant and plosive shortfalls from #37 and #38.

- The noisy-receive condition. `eval/lsd_rx` runs ~4 dB worse than the clean column, which is significantly larger than the difference between models. The objective does not link clean and noisy behavior.
- Whether the 2026-09-13 changes impact primary metrics. The next run (`natural-mod-large-full-ll5`) will be evaluated on `eval/hnr_abs`, plosive columns, and predicted MOS rather than LSD or `eval/hnr`.
- Per-frame tracking. `hnr_abs` has a floor near 0.75 dB but sits at 2.6. If modulation does not improve this, noise gain may need a better conditioning signal than frame features.
- The adversarial path. Implemented but deactivated. It can improve perceptual sharpness at the cost of hallucination, requiring listening tests to evaluate.
- More AMBE capture. Proven to yield returns. YSF/DMR is still in its early stages.
- Untested specialist configurations. A specialist on a smaller profile, new data, or with a higher fine-tuning learning rate remains untested.
